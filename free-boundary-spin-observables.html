Gaussian and non-Gaussian finite-size scalings of the spread-out Ising model at the free-boundary pseudocritical point

Jiwoon Park
November 20, 2026

Manuscript PDF · LaTeX source on GitHub · Research repository

Abstract

For the critical weakly coupled |φ|4 model with periodic boundary conditions, Park [10] showed that only the constant mode has enhanced fluctuations. Motivated by the shifted free-boundary critical window established by Michta, Park, and Slade [13] for the hierarchical model, we study the spread-out Ising model at its free-boundary pseudocritical point. At the upper critical dimension we determine the exponential moments of the spin field tested against smooth real-valued functions vanishing on the boundary. For a nonzero test, the spin sum divided by its standard deviation has a quartic limiting law if the test overlaps the ground state and a standard Gaussian limit if it is orthogonal. In four dimensions the orthogonal susceptibility has order n2 in a box of side n, the scale obtained by Camia, Jiang, and Newman [11] at bulk criticality under a mean-field two-point hypothesis. The lowest sine and the (3, 1, 1, 1) sine have different limiting laws, so it provides a counterevidence to the all-odd classification of Flores-Sola, Berche, Kenna, and Weigel [2].

Main theorem

Fix d ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4} and a sufficiently large spread radius R. Consider the spread-out Ising model with interaction (−ΔR)d/4 in the free box Λ = {1, …, n}d at its pseudocritical inverse temperature. Let F be a nonzero, smooth, real-valued function on the closed unit cube that vanishes on its faces. Write w for the unit ground state of the continuum operator on the cube. Test the spin field with the weighted sum

Xn = (n + 1)−d/2 ∑x ∈ Λ F(x/(n + 1)) σx.

As the side length n tends to infinity, the overlap of F with w determines the variance and limiting law.

  • If ⟨F, w⟩ ≠ 0, the variance has order nd/2√(log n). The spin sum divided by its standard deviation converges in law to the quartic distribution with density proportional to exp(−x4/4), rescaled to unit variance.
  • If ⟨F, w⟩ = 0, the variance has order nd/2 and the spin sum divided by its standard deviation converges in law to a standard Gaussian.
